How it works

Two wallets, one reactive contract, no bots. Here is what happens every time the agent pays for something.

1

The agent pays

It calls spend() on its wallet on Base or Unichain. The wallet checks the approved-payee list and the per-payment cap on the spot.

2

The wallet emits an event

A good payment emits Spent. A bad one doesn't revert. It emits Blocked so the attempt is visible.

3

Reactive reacts

BudgetGuard on Lasna is subscribed to both wallets. Its react() adds every Spent to one total and treats any Blocked as an attack.

4

Callbacks freeze every chain

When a rule breaks, BudgetGuard asks Reactive to call freeze() on every wallet. Each wallet only accepts it from Reactive's callback proxy, sent by BudgetGuard.

The whole system

The limit lives on Reactive Network, not on either chain. That's why a slow drain spread over both chains still gets caught. In the live demo the whole loop, from the blocked payment on Base to freeze callbacks landing on Base and Unichain, took about 2 seconds by block time.

Who can do what

The agent's key is deliberately weak. Even a fully hijacked agent can only spend within the rules.

What it doesn't do

Honest limits, so you know where the edges are.

  • One payment can cross the limit. BudgetGuard sees a payment after it lands, so the payment that takes the total past $50 goes through. The per-payment cap keeps that overshoot small (at most $10 here).
  • It can't stop a bad purchase from an approved payee. If the agent wastes money on an API you approved, that's within the rules until the daily limit.
  • The owner unfreezes by hand. That's on purpose. After an attack, a human should look before the agent spends again.
